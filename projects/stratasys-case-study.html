Stratasys
Feedback Copilot

From discovering a performance gap to building a functional prototype: an on-demand tool that supports managers at the exact moment they need to prepare for a feedback conversation.

In 30 seconds

The problem

Deep workshops existed, but managers still lacked support at the real moment of need — immediately before a feedback conversation.

What we built

Not another course, but a focused performance-support tool with three entry paths and one consistent output structure.

Prototype outcome

The prototype brings three preparation paths into one interface. Each offers practical guidance while the manager remains responsible for the conversation.

01

The need

Stratasys has operated in 3D printing and polymers for roughly 35 years, with around 1,800 employees worldwide. The organization came to us with a clear performance challenge.

What already existed

A strong learning ecosystem

An organizational LMS (SuccessFactors), in-depth training via Zoom, Teams and face-to-face sessions, and a culture of ongoing, immediate feedback alongside a formal review process held twice a year.

What was missing

Support at the moment of performance

A simple tool managers could open immediately before a real conversation — after training had ended, or before it had taken place.

The product, up close

Four mobile views of the functional prototype: a clear starting point, scenario selection, guided output and quick preparation. Opening messages are authored. AI-generated responses in this prototype are labeled placeholders, not live AI output.

03

The process

Before defining the product, our team created a professional identity — Spark, “Ignite Your Learning” — and built a structured discovery process around the client’s real context.

01

Frame

Built the Spark identity — “Ignite Your Learning” — before approaching the client.

02

Discover

Interviewed the client across seven areas: the real problem, audiences, current learning processes, available resources, operational constraints and timelines — and identified the gap between learning events and workplace performance.

03

Define

Translated the insights into product principles, content boundaries and the appropriate product format.

04

Prototype

Wrote a detailed product specification and built a functional AI-assisted mockup with Lovable.

05

Refine

Ran review and QA cycles to improve clarity, consistency and credibility.

04

The solution

The interview revealed that accessibility and simplicity mattered most: a tool that delivers value immediately, without requiring users to learn how to use it first.

THE PRODUCT DECISION Turn existing learning into support that is available when a manager prepares for a real conversation.

01

Start a Chat

A free-form route in which the manager describes the situation in their own words.

02

Choose a Feedback Scenario

A defined route through Positive, Performance, Constructive, or New Employee / Senior Employee feedback.

03

Quick Preparation

A guided four-step process that ends with a personalized conversation map.

05

An ethical framework

Because the product touches manager–employee relationships, we explicitly defined the role and limits of AI rather than leaving them implicit.

01

Support, not replacement

The tool supports professional judgment. It does not replace it.

02

The tool suggests

The manager retains responsibility for the content, tone and final decision.

03

A closed knowledge base

Responses are grounded only in approved organizational learning content.

06

Content and product decisions

Four decisions shaped the product language, its guidance and the way the prototype presents its limits.

01 / GUIDANCE

Guidance built for the moment

Across the three entry paths, we specified a consistent, concise guidance structure: a suggested opening, a clear message, talking points and a common pitfall to avoid. The Quick Preparation screen shows how this structure helps managers scan the guidance before a conversation.

02 / PROTOTYPE HONESTY

An honest prototype

The interface distinguishes authored content from responses that would require a connected AI engine. Those responses are visibly marked as placeholders, so the prototype does not present simulated output as a working AI result.

03 / PRODUCT LANGUAGE

A familiar internal voice

We named the assistant “Feedback Copilot” to fit language already familiar at Stratasys. Its short, direct interface copy was designed to feel like an internal management tool, without emojis or marketing language.

04 / CROSS-DEVICE

One product across devices

The mobile and desktop/LMS views retain the same core routes and visual language, while the navigation adapts to the space available on each screen.

07

Goals and intended impact

The prototype was designed to shorten the distance between knowing how feedback should work and actually conducting a thoughtful conversation.

For managers

Less friction, more confidence

A fast way to apply learning at the moment of truth, organize thoughts, choose an appropriate feedback strategy, and enter the conversation with clear wording and starting points.

For the organization

Learning that reaches the workplace

A scalable layer connecting formal learning with real work, strengthening consistency in feedback language and alignment with organizational values while preserving managerial judgment.

“It is not another tool — it is an upgrade to the organization’s management capabilities.” — Learning Manager, Stratasys

10

Reflection

Understanding the organization deeply — its language, values and daily work — is what turns a generic solution into a truly relevant one.

01

Fit is built in the details

Relevance appeared in every layer: the knowledge base, the product’s tone and even the decision to call it “Copilot.”

02

Human responsibility needs boundaries

In a manager–employee context, the tool must guide rather than dictate. The manager always owns the conversation.

03

Working with AI requires persistence

Even a seemingly small correction, such as preventing a title from wrapping, required repeated prompt and refinement cycles.

04

The next step is evaluation

The project remained a functional mockup and was not tested through an evaluation plan. That would be the essential next stage if the project continued.
